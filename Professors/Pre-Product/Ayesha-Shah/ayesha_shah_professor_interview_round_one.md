## Professor Interview Questions - Round One

**Interviewee:** Ayesha Shah Muhammad

### Question 1

**Question:** Professor [Name], could you briefly introduce yourself for our viewers and mention the subjects and types of classes you teach?

**Answer:**

Ayesha Shah Muhammad.

I am pursuing a PhD in history at the University of the Punjab, Lahore. Professionally, I am teaching as a visiting lecturer at the University of Education Township and the University of Education Lower Mall Campus. In addition, I am working as a visiting lecturer at COMSATS University Lahore. I have two years of teaching experience with BS-level students. ### Question 2

**Question:** What recurring problems require the most effort from you while teaching a class?

**Answer:**

Most of the issues relate to attention span, because students very quickly lose interest and attention. For example, I have often noticed that students lose interest in any topic if it is easy or if the teacher does not give them opportunities to participate. This is a general perception. I do not have any specific examples in mind right now.

### Question 3

**Question:** What learning difficulties do you most commonly observe among students across the class, not only among students who require additional support?

**If clarification is needed, ask about:**

- Memorising without understanding
- Difficulty applying concepts
- Weak foundational knowledge
- Difficulty following instructions
- Poor concentration
- Low participation
- Difficulty retaining previous lessons

**Answer:**

Indeed, all these issues are part of a class but the matter is how a teacher tackles these issues.

From my perspective, the teaching methodology matters here. If the flow of information is one-sided, from the teacher to the students, all the given problems will be in the class. But the teacher can change the strategy at every step by including students through questions and asking about their experiences and visions. Secondly, avoid long and difficult explanations of any concept; just give examples based on the daily routines of common people. Instructions should be clear and in simple wording. Divide the topic into small but linked portions, and after every portion, pause and talk lightly with students. A teacher should use multiple techniques according to the situation, needs, and time.

### Question 4

**Question:** Which teaching methods or learning materials have produced the clearest improvement in your students’ understanding or retention?

**Answer:**

At the university level, the most commonly used teaching methodology is the lecture method, which is used worldwide. At the international level, the problem-solving method, project methods, and multiple uses are applied effectively, but in Pakistan, one issue is related to the design of course content, facilitation of classes, and timetabling. These are not experimental; other methods are difficult to use in a short span of time. I find the lecture method most effective in the scenario of Pakistan, but teachers can use multiple techniques within this method, such as observation, discussion, assignment discussion, article discussion, and current-scenario-based questions, etc. I find these methods effective as well.

### Question 5

**Question:** What changes to teaching or assessment have helped students demonstrate their actual understanding more accurately?

**Answer:**

I think the method of assessment is not a completely aloof thing. Assessment of understanding can be measured through question-and-answer techniques. The teacher goes further only after assessing the previous point. If students follow up to the teacher, then move ahead. Otherwise, use another way to deliver the topic. The purpose is to teach, not just to move on quickly. But if the topic is difficult, it can be broken into two or three parts. Always integrate your current topic with the previous one. Students can demonstrate their understanding through discussion, debates, and verbal responses, not merely in written form. Assessment can be based partially on written work and partially on presentation.

### Question 6

**Question:** When the usual teaching approach does not work for a student, what specific difficulties do you notice, and what support or adjustment has helped?

**Answer:**

When there is a totally new topic unrelated to a student's experience, it becomes a difficult task to teach. But through visuals or multimedia techniques, you can show students videos and images related to that even abstract topic. I find it quite effective. Students were more engaged and curious, but when I taught the same subject through the lecture method, I was sure it would not work.

### Question 7

**Question:** What practical limitations prevent teachers from providing these adjustments or additional support consistently?

**Answer:**

I think there should be flexibility in course content and fully equipped classrooms because without institutional support, effective learning is not possible.

### Question 8

**Question:** Based on your own experience with online classes, what do you think is the biggest problem with online learning for students and teachers?

**Answer:**

I have had a very bad experience with online classes because students don’t take this mode of learning seriously, and at the BS level, I think this mode is not suitable either. At this level, students learn the basic concepts of the course, and the poor condition of the internet in Pakistan is a hurdle. I think this mode should be used at a more mature level, with the facilitation of the institution.
