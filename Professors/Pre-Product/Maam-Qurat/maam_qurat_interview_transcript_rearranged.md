## 4. Common Learning Difficulties Observed

**[03:24] Alizay:** Right. So, Ma'am, have you observed any learning disabilities among the students that are the most common?

**[03:36] Ma'am Qurat:** Memory. Memory issues. They don't remember. Memorizing is very difficult for students. And applying theory to practical problems. I think this is, again, a general, common problem here—that we have been turned into rote learners. ## 5. Teaching Methods Used to Improve Focus & Engagement

**[05:06] Alizay:** Okay, Ma'am. So, regarding all these issues, are there any teaching methods you use that you feel are helpful in keeping the children focused and engaged?

**[05:19] Ma'am Qurat:** Yes. I prefer—first of all, my method is brainstorming. It's a technique... whenever I start a new topic, because look, IT right now is the only field that is embedded in all other fields. So even in this era, if you ask a layman to speak on IT, he or she can speak very easily. Usually, when I start my topic, before that, I take the students' opinions, ideas, and views regarding that topic. And ultimately, somewhere around the 30th student in a class of 30, they come up with the answer. Not exactly perfectly, but closely.

Besides that, to resolve the issues I mentioned earlier and to improve their memory, I sometimes prefer group discussions. I make small groups, give them topics, and I ask them whatever the first opinion or idea comes into their head. I usually tell them that it's a natural phenomenon: whenever you hear a term, a concept immediately develops in your mind about it, whether it's right or wrong. Regardless of whether that idea is right or wrong, upon seeing anything or hearing anything unknown, our brain generates its own opinion about it. So I usually ask them to share that very first idea that comes to their mind. Then, slowly, we refine it.

Similarly, I prefer group discussions. Forming small groups, writing down your thoughts, discussing them among yourselves, and as a result of that discussion, telling me the final analysis.

In addition to that, practical demonstrations happen. Because sometimes students, as I told you, even in this era, there are still students who don't have PCs or laptops in their homes, and they don't even know how to operate them. So for that, there are practical demonstrations on how to work with these gadgets, and a general explanation is given to them about how these things work, what kind of components are inside them, and how they interact with each other, and all that.

Besides that, I use multimedia presentations. This is one of the ways students will work themselves, and finally, they will self-research on any topic and will definitely arrive at a conclusive point. So, these are some methods that are implemented to try and solve the students' problems.

## 6. Changes to Teaching & Assessment Methods

**[08:37] Alizay:** Right, Ma'am. So, did you ever have to make any changes to your teaching or assessment methods to better facilitate the students?

**[08:50] Ma'am Qurat:** Changes... as such, it's just the things I mentioned to you: active learning, brainstorming. And if the whole class is at the same status—meaning the whole class isn't able to understand the concepts and it's becoming problematic for everyone—then I usually don't prefer to keep teaching them theoretically. I stop teaching and I start giving them tasks, at a basic level, because until your basics are clear, you can't move forward. I usually try to help them with presentations and quizzes. I announce that I'll mark the quiz, but I don't actually mark it after taking it, just so that the feeling of relaxation is there later, and by reading for it, they at least concentrate a bit. Besides that, grace marks... I give them, you know, the way we tell kids, "Do this work and you'll get a gift," I usually try to apply that strategy for university students. I announce in the first lecture that you will participate in class, you will answer, you will be actively involved—so you will be given grace marks. The student who actively participates—because sometimes it happens that brilliant students are there but they are not serious, they don't want to study. And some kids have the mindset of, "We know everything, we don't need to study." So when incentives like this are discussed, like getting grace marks, getting extra separate marks, those who will participate, those who will join in all the activities... I have observed that this is also an activity through which you can engage the maximum class.
